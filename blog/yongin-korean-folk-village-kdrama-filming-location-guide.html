Korean Folk Village in Yongin: K-Drama Filming Location Guide (100 Days My Prince, Tale of the Nine-Tailed)

The Korean Folk Village (한국민속촌) in Yongin, a recreated late-Joseon village about an hour from Seoul, has 15 records in Korea's public KCISA filming dataset. This guide shows which scenes of 100 Days My Prince, Tale of the Nine-Tailed, The King's Affection, Dae Jang Geum, Kingdom and more were filmed there, and how to get there by direct bus or via Sanggal Station.

Why the Korean Folk Village Is K-Drama's Favorite Joseon Set

The Korean Folk Village (한국민속촌, Hanguk Minsokchon) in Giheung-gu, Yongin, is an open-air museum and theme park that recreates life in the late Joseon Dynasty. Opened in 1974, it gathers farmhouses, a market street, a yangban (aristocrat) estate, a local government office, a Confucian school, shrines, mills and workshops from different regions of Korea into one walkable village, with a stream and wooden bridges running through the middle.

For drama producers, that makes it a ready-made Joseon town with no power lines, apartment blocks or neon signs in the frame, and only about an hour from Seoul. Korea's public KCISA filming-location dataset (한국문화정보원 미디어 촬영지 데이터, published via 공공데이터포털) lists 15 records at the Korean Folk Village (경기도 용인시 기흥구 민속촌로 90): 9 dramas, 1 film and 5 K-pop artist entries. Many sageuk fans will recognize one spot in particular, the village's wooden bridge, which four different dramas in the dataset use.

This guide covers only the Korean Folk Village: which scenes the public data places here, what to look for as you walk, the exact Korean address for a taxi, how to get there by bus or subway from Seoul and Suwon, where to eat, and tips for a smooth visit.

What Was Filmed at the Korean Folk Village (Public Data)

Everything below comes from the KCISA records for 한국민속촌. Where a record is short or vague, we only repeat what it says and do not add scene details we cannot verify.

The wooden bridge: four dramas, one crossing

The village's wooden bridge (나무다리) is the most-repeated location in the dataset. In 100 Days My Prince (백일의 낭군님), it is the bridge where Hong-shim (Nam Ji-hyun) waits for her brother once a month, keeping a promise they made ten years earlier. The record adds that in episode 2 this is where Jeong Je-yoon (Kim Seon-ho) and the adult Lee Yul (D.O. of EXO) first see Hong-shim, and that in episode 5 Je-yoon prepares the floating lanterns that make her wish come true.

In The King's Affection (연모), episode 1, the young Jung Ji-woon (Go Woo-rim) waits on the wooden bridge for the young Dam-i (Choi Myung-bin), as they had arranged, but she never comes. In Arang and the Magistrate (아랑사또전), episode 2, an irritated and frustrated Arang (Shin Min-a) crosses the same bridge, and in The Crowned Clown (왕이 된 남자), episode 1, Ha-seon (Yeo Jin-goo) and his troupe of performers leap across it.

Tale of the Nine-Tailed (구미호뎐) brings the bridge into the present day. In episode 5, Lee Yeon (Lee Dong-wook) and Nam Ji-ah (Jo Bo-ah) visit the folk village together. Ji-ah sees a hanbok in a shop and decides to wear it, and when Lee Yeon finds her in it he is pulled straight back into his memories of A-eum. He spots her on the wooden bridge and runs to kiss her.

Palace physicians, plagues and punishments

The village has also stood in for ordinary Joseon towns in harder times. In Dae Jang Geum (대장금, also known as Jewel in the Palace), Jang-geum (Lee Young-ae) cares for smallpox patients in this village in episode 51, and in episode 54 she gives birth to Min Jeong-ho's (Ji Jin-hee) child here. In Iljimae (일지매), the dataset says the village was used to film a settlement struck by an epidemic.

The Netflix series Kingdom (킹덤) is also listed, with the record simply describing the Korean Folk Village as a representative traditional-culture theme park and a Kingdom filming location. For Hwarang (화랑), the record notes a scene of Choi Min-ho's character talking with his younger sibling.

The only film in the dataset is Masquerade (광해, 왕이 된 남자, 2012), where the record notes the spot where Lee Byung-hun's character is flogged (곤장).

K-pop visits

Five artist entries round out the list. Kang Daniel visited in episode 5 of his travel log series, Stray Kids filmed episode 5 of Finding SKZ here, Apink's Bomi and Hayoung came to the park, Dreamcatcher's Dami visited, and g.o.d's Park Joon-hyung stopped by for the web show Wassup Man. The dataset does not give the exact corners for these visits, so treat the whole village as fair game for recreating their photos.

Walking the Village: Bridge, Government Office and Yangban Estate

The village is large, and many visitors spend three to five hours here. A simple loop that covers the drama spots looks like this.

The stream and the wooden bridges

Follow the stream that runs through the middle of the village. This is where the bridge scenes from 100 Days My Prince, The King's Affection, Arang and the Magistrate, The Crowned Clown and Tale of the Nine-Tailed were shot. Look for a simple wooden footbridge over the water framed by willows and thatched roofs; on a calm day you get a reflection that looks straight out of a sageuk opening scene.

The government office (관아)

The local magistrate's office, with its tiled gate, courtyard and raised hall, is the kind of set where Joseon trials and punishments play out. Fans of Masquerade and of magistrate dramas like Arang and the Magistrate will recognize the layout.

Farmhouses, the yangban estate and the market street

Thatched farmhouses from different provinces, including a Jeju-style lane with black stone walls, fill much of the village. Further in, the yangban estate shows how wealthy families lived, with tiled roofs, inner courtyards and pavilions. The market street is where the village feels most like a drama town, with low tile-roofed stalls and food counters.

Performances and seasonal events

The village runs traditional performances, such as farmers' music (nongak), tightrope walking and equestrian shows, and it often adds seasonal festivals and night events. Schedules change by season and day of the week, so check the timetable at the entrance or on the official site and plan your bridge photos around the show times when the crowds move to the arena.

Address, Hours and How to Get There

Exact address for a taxi

Show this to the driver or paste it into Naver Map, KakaoMap or Kakao T: 경기도 용인시 기흥구 민속촌로 90 (90 Minsokchon-ro, Giheung-gu, Yongin-si, Gyeonggi-do), or simply 한국민속촌. The phone number in the public data is 031-288-0000.

Opening hours and tickets

The KCISA data lists the village as open daily from 10:00 to 18:00. In practice the park adjusts its closing time and operating days by season and day of the week, and admission is paid, with prices that differ between weekdays, weekends and special events. Check the current hours and prices on the official website, koreanfolk.co.kr, before you set out.

Direct bus from Seoul

The village's official transport guide lists three direct buses from Seoul: 5001-1 from Sinnonhyeon Station via Gangnam and Yangjae, 1560 from Gangnam Station (get off at 민속촌 입구, Folk Village Entrance), and 4101 from the Sungnyemun / Seoul Station area. From Incheon Airport, the airport bus A8877 also stops at the village. These are the easiest options if your hotel is near one of the starting points.

Subway plus local bus

The nearest subway station is Sanggal Station (상갈역) on the Suin-Bundang Line. Leave by Exit 3 and take bus 37 or 10-5 to the Korean Folk Village stop. From Suwon Station (Line 1 and KTX), leave by Exit 4 and take bus 37 or 10-5. From Jukjeon Station on the Suin-Bundang Line, bus 30 also runs to the village. A taxi from Sanggal Station is a short ride of roughly 10 minutes.

Getting back

Return buses can be infrequent in the evening. Save the return stop in your map app and check the last bus on Naver Map or KakaoMap before the park closes, or plan to take a taxi back to Sanggal or Suwon Station.

Where to Eat Near the Korean Folk Village

Inside the village. The village market street (장터) is the classic lunch stop, with Korean snacks and simple meals such as savory pancakes (pajeon), rice soups and rice-cake treats served at low wooden tables. Eating here also makes for a very drama-like photo.

A samgyetang restaurant from public data. The KCISA dataset lists 이창노녹두삼계탕메기매운탕 민속촌본점, featured on SBS's Morning Wide (episode 7080) for its mung-bean samgyetang (ginseng chicken soup). It is about 1 km from the village at 경기도 용인시 기흥구 사은로 74 (보리마을), and the record lists it as open Tuesday to Sunday from 09:00 to 22:00 and closed on Mondays. Restaurant details can change, so confirm on Naver Map before going.

Make it a culture day. The Nam June Paik Art Center (백남준아트센터, 경기도 용인시 기흥구 백남준로 10) is about 1.5 km away and appears in the dataset for a visit by ATEEZ members Wooyoung, Seonghwa and Hongjoong. The public data lists it as open Tuesday to Sunday from 10:00 to 18:00 and closed on Mondays, New Year's Day and Lunar holidays. It pairs well with a morning at the folk village.

Visit Tips: Best Time, Photos and Etiquette

Arrive at opening. School groups and tour buses arrive mid-morning. If you want the wooden bridge without a crowd, head there first and save the market street and performances for later.

Seasons. Spring blossoms and autumn leaves (usually late October to early November) are the most photogenic, and the stream reflects the color beautifully. Summer is hot with little shade on the open lanes. Winter is cold, but snow on thatched roofs looks like a sageuk winter episode.

Wear (or rent) a hanbok. Like Nam Ji-ah in Tale of the Nine-Tailed, many visitors walk the village in hanbok. Check the official site or on-site signage for current rental options and any hanbok-related ticket offers before you go.

Plan a full day. Counting the bus or subway ride, a visit easily fills most of a day. Bring comfortable shoes, because the paths are mostly dirt and gravel.

Respect the place. The houses are part of a museum: do not climb walls or roofs, stay out of roped-off rooms, ask before photographing performers and staff up close, and keep drones grounded unless you have permission.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which K-dramas were filmed at the Korean Folk Village?

The KCISA public dataset lists 9 dramas at the Korean Folk Village in Yongin: 100 Days My Prince, Tale of the Nine-Tailed, The King's Affection, The Crowned Clown, Arang and the Magistrate, Dae Jang Geum, Iljimae, Kingdom and Hwarang, plus the film Masquerade and visits by Kang Daniel, Stray Kids, Apink, Dreamcatcher and g.o.d.

Where is the wooden bridge from 100 Days My Prince?

According to the KCISA dataset, the wooden bridge where Hong-shim (Nam Ji-hyun) waits for her brother is at the Korean Folk Village, 경기도 용인시 기흥구 민속촌로 90. The same bridge appears in The King's Affection, Arang and the Magistrate, The Crowned Clown and Tale of the Nine-Tailed.

What is the address of the Korean Folk Village for a taxi?

Show 경기도 용인시 기흥구 민속촌로 90 or simply 한국민속촌 to the driver, or paste it into Naver Map, KakaoMap or Kakao T.

How do I get to the Korean Folk Village from Seoul?

Take a direct bus (5001-1 from Sinnonhyeon or Gangnam, 1560 from Gangnam, or 4101 from the Seoul Station area), or ride the Suin-Bundang Line to Sanggal Station, leave by Exit 3 and take bus 37 or 10-5. From Suwon Station Exit 4, buses 37 and 10-5 also go to the village.

What are the opening hours and is there an admission fee?

The public data lists 10:00 to 18:00 daily, but the park adjusts hours and operating days by season. Admission is paid and prices differ by day and season, so check koreanfolk.co.kr before visiting.
