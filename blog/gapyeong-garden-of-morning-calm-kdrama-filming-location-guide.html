Garden of Morning Calm in Gapyeong: K-Drama Filming Location Guide (Love in the Moonlight, She Was Pretty)

The Garden of Morning Calm (아침고요수목원) in Gapyeong, a mountain arboretum about 90 minutes from Seoul, has 14 records in Korea's public KCISA filming dataset. This guide shows which scenes of Love in the Moonlight, She Was Pretty, Now, We Are Breaking Up, Perfume, Navillera and more were filmed there, and how to get there via Cheongpyeong Station and bus 30-6.

Why the Garden of Morning Calm Keeps Showing Up in K-Dramas

The Garden of Morning Calm (아침고요수목원, Achim Goyo Sumogwon) is a privately run arboretum on the slopes of Chungnyeongsan mountain in Sang-myeon, Gapyeong-gun, about an hour and a half east of central Seoul. According to the garden's own history page, it was founded by horticulture professor Han Sang-kyung, who started work on the site in 1994, and it opened on 11 May 1996. The name comes from the poet Rabindranath Tagore's description of Korea as the “land of the morning calm.” Today the garden covers about 330,000 square meters and has more than 20 themed gardens, with roughly 5,000 plant species.

For drama directors it is a dependable romantic set. Wherever the camera points you get flowers, curving paths, mountains in the background, and nothing modern in the frame. Korea's public KCISA filming-location dataset (한국문화정보원 미디어 촬영지 데이터, published on 공공데이터포털) lists 14 records at the Garden of Morning Calm (경기도 가평군 상면 수목원로 432): 11 dramas, 1 variety show and 2 K-pop artist visits. They range from a royal palace garden in Love in the Moonlight to Song Hye-kyo's photo shoot in Now, We Are Breaking Up.

This guide covers only the Garden of Morning Calm. You'll find which scenes the public data places here, what to look for as you walk, the exact Korean address for a taxi, how to get there by train and bus from Seoul, where to eat, and practical visit tips.

What Was Filmed at the Garden of Morning Calm (Public Data)

Every entry below comes from the KCISA dataset's scene descriptions. Episode numbers follow the dataset. Some broadcasters split episodes in two, so the numbers may not match your streaming service.

Romance in full bloom

Love in the Moonlight (구르미 그린 달빛, 2016): The garden stands in for part of the royal palace. In episode 1, Crown Prince Lee Yeong (Park Bo-gum) snatches a love letter from his younger sister, Princess Myeong-eun, while she is happily eating beside him. In episode 3, Hong Ra-on (Kim Yoo-jung) is gardening here with a young princess when the prince comes over. The two bicker for a while before he calls Ra-on his “friend.” (If you love this drama, our Gwanghallu Garden guide covers another of its locations.)

She Was Pretty (그녀는 예뻤다, 2015): In the final episode 16, Kim Hye-jin (Hwang Jung-eum) and Ji Sung-joon (Park Seo-joon) come to this garden for a picnic.

Now, We Are Breaking Up (지금, 헤어지는 중입니다, 2021): In episode 13, Ha Young-eun (Song Hye-kyo) does a fashion photo shoot in the garden with Yoon Jae-guk (Jang Ki-yong).

Perfume (퍼퓸, 2019): The garden is the drama's dream setting. In episode 1, Min Jae-hee puts on a mysterious perfume and sees herself in a beautiful, peaceful garden. Later, Seo Yi-do (Shin Sung-rok) dreams of dancing with her here, and the dataset says the two finally visit the real garden together and share their first kiss.

Mary Stayed Out All Night (매리는 외박중, 2010): Jung-in (Kim Jae-wook) brings Mary (Moon Geun-young) here because she once said she loves places like this.

Your House Helper (당신의 하우스헬퍼, 2018): In episode 28, Kim Ji-woon (Ha Seok-jin) meets Im Da-young (Bona) for their first date, and she tells him this is where her father rests.

I'm Not a Robot (로봇이 아니야, 2017): In episode 12, Sun-hye teaches Kim Min-kyu (Yoo Seung-ho) a thing or two about dating in this garden.

Jugglers (저글러스, 2017): In the closing scene of episode 16, Nam Chi-won (Daniel Choi) and Jwa Yoon-yi (Baek Jin-hee) take a walk here.

Farewells and memories

Several dramas use the garden's peaceful hillsides as a place of remembrance. In Navillera (나빌레라, 2021) episode 2, it is where the close friend of Shim Deok-chul (Park In-hwan) has been laid to rest, and Deok-chul visits to tell him he has started ballet. In School 2017 (학교 2017), the dataset places the resting place of Hyun Tae-woon's (Kim Jung-hyun) mother here. In episode 18 of the OCN thriller Black (블랙, 2017), an earlier version of Kang Ha-ram (Go Ara) dies on a bench here, holding Jun's bracelet.

Variety shows and K-pop

Korean variety fans may know the garden from Infinite Challenge (무한도전). The dataset says Park Myung-soo's famous “woodpecker” gag was born here during episode 24's autumn picnic. For K-pop, the dataset records Apink members Chorong, Eunji and Namjoo visiting the arboretum, and INFINITE's Dongwoo taking a “healing time” break here in episode 2 of a travel show.

The dataset does not name the exact flower bed or bench for each scene. Treat the garden as a whole as the location, and look for the open lawns, flower terraces and Korean-style garden corners that match the scenes you remember.

Walking the Garden: Flower Terraces, Korean Garden and the Old Juniper

Most visitors spend two to four hours here. The paths are well signed, and the official site has a downloadable guide map (종합안내도). A relaxed loop past the most drama-like scenery looks like this.

Hakyeong Garden and the open lawns

The Hakyeong Garden (하경정원) is one of the garden's original themed gardens. It is a wide terrace of curving flower beds that looks out over the valley, and in spring and summer this is where the classic picnic-and-stroll shots happen. The open lawn nearby is easy to match to the light romantic scenes from She Was Pretty, Jugglers and Mary Stayed Out All Night.

The Korean Garden (Seohwayeon)

The Korean Garden (한국정원, also called 서화연 Seohwayeon) has traditional hanok-style buildings, a pond and a small pavilion. It is the corner that feels most like a palace garden, so fans of Love in the Moonlight will want to linger here. The garden's Chorok Sangjeom shop (초록상점), inside this area, sells flower tea and coffee.

Cheonnyeonhyang, the thousand-year juniper

Look for Cheonnyeonhyang (천년향), an old juniper with a wide, cloud-like crown that the garden lists among its signature natural sights. It makes a striking photo backdrop, and many visitors queue for a shot in front of it.

More themed gardens and greenhouses

Other areas include the Secret Garden (비밀의정원), the hillside Sky Road (하늘길) and a bonsai garden, plus indoor greenhouses that are handy on rainy or cold days.

Address, Hours and How to Get There

Exact address for a taxi

Show this to the driver or paste it into Naver Map, KakaoMap or Kakao T: 경기도 가평군 상면 수목원로 432 (432 Sumogwon-ro, Sang-myeon, Gapyeong-gun, Gyeonggi-do), or simply 아침고요수목원. The main phone number is 1544-6703.

Opening hours and tickets

The official website lists the garden as open every day of the year from 08:30 to 19:00, with last entry at 18:00. During the winter light festival (오색별빛정원전), which the garden says runs from early December to mid-March, hours shift into the evening, so check the current schedule before you go. When we checked in November 2026, the official price list showed adult admission at 11,000 won, youth at 8,500 won and children at 7,500 won. Tickets are sold on site only. Prices and hours can change, so confirm on morningcalm.co.kr before you set out.

Train from Seoul to Cheongpyeong

The nearest rail hub is Cheongpyeong Station (청평역). Take the Gyeongchun Line subway, or the faster ITX-Cheongchun train from Yongsan or Cheongnyangni Station. Intercity buses also run to Cheongpyeong Terminal from Dong Seoul Terminal and from the Cheongnyangni transfer center.

Local bus 30-6 or a taxi

From Cheongpyeong Terminal or Cheongpyeong Station, take local bus 30-6 to the last stop at the arboretum. The official transport page shows departures roughly every 30 to 60 minutes through the day. A taxi from central Cheongpyeong takes about 20 minutes without traffic.

Gapyeong City Tour Bus

The Gapyeong City Tour Bus loops between major Gapyeong sights, including the garden, and stops at Cheongpyeong Terminal and Station. The official site lists a one-day pass at 8,000 won for adults and notes that the bus does not run on Wednesdays, with separate peak and off-peak timetables.

Getting back

The last buses back to Cheongpyeong leave in the evening, and weekend roads around Gapyeong get very busy. Save the return stop in your map app and check the last 30-6 departure before you start exploring.

Where to Eat at and Near the Garden of Morning Calm

You can eat without leaving the garden. The official store guide lists Food Garden (푸드가든) in the bonsai garden area, which serves Korean dishes such as doenjang stew, bibimbap with seasoned vegetables and mushroom hot pot. There is also Good Morning Coffee (굿모닝커피), a roasting house in a hanok under the pine trees, the Achim Bom bakery (아침봄빵집) for bread and madeleines, and a small snack shop. Opening times and closing days vary by outlet, so check the store page on the official site.

Outside the garden, Gapyeong is known for dakgalbi (spicy stir-fried chicken) and dishes made with local pine nuts (잣, jat), such as pine-nut noodles and porridge. Restaurants cluster around Cheongpyeong and along the road to the garden. Search “가평 잣” or “청평 닭갈비” on Naver Map or KakaoMap to see what is open nearby and read recent local reviews.

Visit Tips: Best Time, Photos and Etiquette

Pick your season. Spring brings azaleas and wildflowers, early summer brings irises and hydrangeas, and autumn color on the mountain usually peaks from late October into early November. From early December, the winter light festival fills the gardens with colored lights after dark, which makes it a popular date spot, just like on screen.

Go early on weekdays. Weekend traffic to Gapyeong can be heavy, and tour buses arrive mid-morning. Arriving at opening gives you empty paths for photos.

Dress for the mountain. The garden sits in a mountain valley and is noticeably cooler than Seoul, especially for the evening light festival. Wear comfortable shoes, because some paths are sloped.

Know the rules. The official visitor rules say pets are not allowed and that the whole garden is non-smoking and alcohol-free. Drones are banned, and so is stepping into flower beds for photos. Re-entry after leaving is not allowed, and tickets are not refunded because of rain. Stay on the marked paths and do not pick plants.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which K-dramas were filmed at the Garden of Morning Calm?

The KCISA public dataset lists 11 dramas at the Garden of Morning Calm in Gapyeong: Love in the Moonlight, She Was Pretty, Now, We Are Breaking Up, Perfume, Navillera, Mary Stayed Out All Night, Your House Helper, I'm Not a Robot, Jugglers, Black and School 2017. It also lists the variety show Infinite Challenge and visits by Apink and INFINITE's Dongwoo.

Was Love in the Moonlight filmed at the Garden of Morning Calm?

Yes. According to the KCISA dataset, the garden stands in for part of the royal palace in Love in the Moonlight. In episode 1, Crown Prince Lee Yeong (Park Bo-gum) snatches a love letter from his sister here, and in episode 3, Hong Ra-on (Kim Yoo-jung) is gardening here when he comes over.

What is the address of the Garden of Morning Calm for a taxi?

Show 경기도 가평군 상면 수목원로 432 or simply 아침고요수목원 to the driver, or paste it into Naver Map, KakaoMap or Kakao T.

How do I get to the Garden of Morning Calm from Seoul?

Take the Gyeongchun Line or the ITX-Cheongchun train (from Yongsan or Cheongnyangni) to Cheongpyeong Station, or an intercity bus to Cheongpyeong Terminal. From there, take local bus 30-6 to the last stop at the arboretum, or a taxi of about 20 minutes. The Gapyeong City Tour Bus also stops at the garden, except on Wednesdays.

What are the opening hours and admission fee?

The official site lists 08:30 to 19:00 every day, with last entry at 18:00. Hours change during the winter light festival (from early December to mid-March). When we checked in November 2026, adult admission was 11,000 won, and tickets are sold on site only. Check morningcalm.co.kr for current hours and prices before visiting.
