Busan Suyeong-gu (Gwangalli, Gwangan Bridge, Millak, F1963): K-Drama & K-Pop Filming Spots Guide

Suyeong-gu has 39 records in Korea's public KCISA filming-location dataset, centered on Gwangalli Beach and Gwangan Bridge, which appear in D.P., Sisyphus: The Myth, Dance Sports Girls, Shopping King Louie and music videos by Apink and BTOB. This guide links the beach, the bridge views, Millak waterfront, Namcheon-dong and F1963 into one easy subway day.

Why Suyeong-gu Is Busan's Bridge-and-Beach Screen Set

If Haeundae is Busan's postcard, Suyeong-gu is the view that directors cut to when a story needs to say "we are in Busan now." The district wraps around Gwangalli Beach, a curve of sand about 1.4 km long facing Gwangan Bridge, the double-deck suspension bridge that crosses the bay toward Haeundae. Korea's public KCISA filming-location dataset lists 39 records with a Suyeong-gu address: 15 from idol and artist visits, 15 from variety and food shows, 8 from TV dramas and 1 from a film. Two places do most of the work: Gwangalli Beach appears in 13 records and Gwangan Bridge in 10.

That concentration makes Suyeong-gu one of the easiest filming areas in Korea to visit. Busan Metro Line 2 runs right behind the beach, with Geumnyeonsan, Gwangan and Suyeong stations all within walking distance of the sand, and most food spots in the dataset sit within a few blocks of the promenade. You can see almost everything in a single afternoon and evening.

Gwangalli Beach: Dance Sports Girls, Shopping King Louie and Let's Eat 3

Gwangalli Beach (광안리해수욕장, 부산광역시 수영구 광안해변로 219) is open around the clock, with official swimming in July and August. In episode 11 of Dance Sports Girls, the high-school dance team takes a trip here and poses for a group photo at the far eastern end of the beach; that evening their two teachers drink on the sand and one asks the other to take over the club. In episode 5 of Shopping King Louie, Go Bok-sil, Louie and Jo In-sung arrive at the beach on their Busan trip, and Let's Eat 3 also shot scenes on the sand and against the bridge.

The 2016 film Will You Be There? uses Gwangalli to show the lasting friendship between the present-day Soo-hyun and Tae-ho. For K-pop fans, the dataset records SEVENTEEN filming their 'Adore U' part-switch video here, Stray Kids' Seungmin posting the beach on Instagram, and visits by Taeyang, fromis_9's Jiwon and Chaeyoung, Block B's Jaehyo and ATEEZ.

Getting there

Take Line 2 to Gwangan Station and use Exit 3 or 5; it is about a 7 minute walk downhill to the beach. For a taxi, show the driver 광안리해수욕장 or the address 광안해변로 219.

Gwangan Bridge: D.P., Sisyphus and Music Video Night Views

Gwangan Bridge (광안대교) is the single most-filmed structure in the district. In episode 3 of D.P., An Jun-ho and Han Ho-yeol arrive in Busan and ride a Busan City Tour bus across it. In episode 3 of Sisyphus: The Myth, Kang Seo-hae crosses the bridge in a taxi. In episode 1 of Now, We Are Breaking Up, Hwang Chi-sook drives over it on the way to call her model boyfriend, and in episode 1 of Fates & Furies, Goo Hae-ra brings an Italian businessman here.

The bridge is also a music video favorite. Apink's 'Remember' and BTOB's 'It's Okay Without You' (너 없인 안 된다) both used it, and BTS's Jimin posted a finger-heart photo with the bridge on Twitter.

Best viewpoints

The classic view is from the middle of Gwangalli Beach after sunset, when the bridge lights change color. For a lower, quieter angle, walk north to Millak Waterside Park (민락수변공원) at the eastern end of the beach, where the bridge passes close overhead. Busan City Tour buses on some routes cross the upper deck, which is the easiest way to recreate the D.P. ride without a car. The Busan Fireworks Festival is also held over this bay each autumn; check the official date before you plan around it.

Busking, Pizza and Raw Fish on the Promenade

Gwangalli's promenade is one of Busan's main busking stages, and on clear weekend evenings you will usually find singers set up facing the bridge. Food in the dataset is clustered here too. Mad Dogs (매드독스, 광안해변로 235), a Chicago-style deep-dish pizza place right on the beachfront, appears in Baek Jong-won's Top 3 Chef King, Delicious Rendezvous and Siksin Namnyeo, and NU'EST's Ren visited; the dataset lists it as open daily from noon to midnight.

For something more local, Gaemijip Gwangalli (개미집 광안리직영점, 광남로130번길 9), the nakji-bokkeum (spicy stir-fried octopus) chain Simon Dominic recommended on Wednesday Food Talk, opens daily from 11am to 10pm. Toward Millak, Pungseong Galbi & Jogaegui (풍성갈비&조개구이, 민락수변로 7) serves grilled shellfish from 4pm to midnight. Millak's raw fish market buildings sit beside the waterside park if you want sashimi with a bridge view.

Namcheon-dong and F1963: Squid Tempura, Bingsu and a Wire Factory

West of the beach, Namcheon-dong is a quieter residential neighborhood known for its cherry-blossom apartment roads in spring. The dataset lists Dari-jip (다리집 본점, 남천바다로10번길 70), a squid tempura and tteokbokki shop visited by Son Dam-bi, open Wednesday to Monday, and Namcheon Green Tea Patbingsu (남천녹차팥빙수, 수영로394번길 28), featured on Live Today, for shaved ice. Namcheon Station on Line 2 is the closest stop.

F1963

North of Suyeong Station, F1963 (복합문화공간 F1963, 구락로123번길 20) is a former wire-rope factory turned into a culture complex with a bookstore, cafes, galleries and gardens. Hong Jin-ho and Lee Na-woo busked here together on Begin Again Korea. The dataset lists it as open daily from 9am to 9pm. The nearest subway stop is Mangmi Station on Line 3, about a 15 minute walk, so many visitors take a short taxi from Suyeong Station.

Nearby, Yeongjin Dwaeji Gukbap (영진돼지국밥 코스트코센텀점, 구락로 86) is where Running Man episode 328 ate pork-and-rice soup with TWICE, and Suyeong Dwaeji Gukbap (수영 돼지국밥, 무학로 6), recommended by NCT's Johnny on Wednesday Food Talk, opens from 8am.

Hwangnyeongsan and a Half-Day Suyeong-gu Itinerary

Hwangnyeongsan (황령산) rises on Suyeong-gu's western edge, and its summit area gives one of the widest night views of Busan, including Gwangan Bridge. Mugunghwa (무궁화, 황령산로8번길 5), a dumpling hot-pot restaurant with a signature from Shinhwa's Minwoo, sits at the foot of the mountain road. The summit is easiest by taxi.

A simple half day: start at F1963 around 2pm for coffee and the bookstore, take a taxi to Namcheon-dong for squid tempura at Dari-jip, then walk east along the beach from Geumnyeonsan Station toward Gwangan. Watch the sunset from the sand, eat pizza or grilled shellfish, and finish at Millak Waterside Park as the bridge lights come on. Allow about five hours. Restaurants change, so confirm hours on Naver Map or KakaoMap before you go.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which K-dramas were filmed at Gwangan Bridge?

Korea's public KCISA dataset lists D.P. (episode 3 city tour bus), Sisyphus: The Myth (episode 3 taxi ride), Now, We Are Breaking Up (episode 1), Fates & Furies (episode 1) and Let's Eat 3, plus music videos by Apink and BTOB.

How do I get to Gwangalli Beach from Busan Station?

Take Busan Metro Line 1 to Seomyeon, transfer to Line 2 toward Jangsan and get off at Gwangan Station. The trip takes about 35 to 40 minutes, then it is a 7 minute walk to the beach.

Can I walk across Gwangan Bridge?

No. Gwangan Bridge is a motorway for cars only, except on special event days. The best way to cross is a taxi or a Busan City Tour bus route that uses the bridge.

When is Gwangan Bridge lit up?

The bridge is lit every evening after sunset. The view is best from the middle of Gwangalli Beach or from Millak Waterside Park at the eastern end.

Is F1963 free to visit?

Entry to the F1963 grounds is free. Individual shops, cafes and some exhibitions inside charge separately, and the dataset lists the complex as open daily from 9am to 9pm.
