Why Jongno & Bukchon Are the Beating Heart of K-Drama Cinematography

No district in South Korea appears on global streaming screens more frequently than Jongno-gu and neighboring Jung-gu in central Seoul. Verified records from the Korea Cultural Information Service Agency (KCISA) public filming dataset show hundreds of drama, film, and music video productions clustering around Gyeongbokgung Palace, Changdeokgung Palace, Bukchon Hanok Village, Seochon, and Deoksugung Stone Wall Road.

For international travelers, what makes Jongno extraordinary is its walkable density. Within a single two-kilometer radius, you can stand on the ginkgo-lined stone path where Kim Shin first passed Ji Eun-tak under a rain umbrella in Goblin (Guardian: The Lonely and Great God), climb the curved traditional tiled-roof ridge of Bukchon Hanok Village overlooking modern Seoul towers, and step into the nostalgic neighborhood alleys of Seochon and Ikseon-dong.

Key Verified Filming Locations in This District (With Exact Korean Addresses)

1. Deoksugung Stone Wall Road (덕수궁 돌담길 — 99 Sejong-daero, Jung-gu, Seoul): Running along the outer wall of Deoksugung Palace from City Hall Station (Subway Line 1 & 2, Exit 1 or 2), this 900-meter pedestrian lane is legendary in Goblin, Do You Like Brahms?, and Lovely Runner. Visit between 8:00 AM and 9:30 AM for soft morning light filtering through the trees, or during late October and November when golden ginkgo leaves carpet the cobblestones.

2. Bukchon Hanok Village Photo Point #6 (북촌한옥마을 — 37 Gyedong-gil, Jongno-gu, Seoul): Reached via Anguk Station (Line 3, Exit 2), this uphill residential lane preserves authentic Joseon-era aristocratic homes (hanok). Stand at the top of the slope looking south to capture dark curved clay roofs framing Namsan Seoul Tower in the distance. Remember that Bukchon is an active residential neighborhood with designated visitor hours (10:00 AM–5:00 PM) and quiet-zone etiquette.

3. Gyeongbokgung Palace & Hyangwonjeong Pavilion (경복궁 — 161 Sajik-ro, Jongno-gu, Seoul): Accessed directly from Gyeongbokgung Station (Line 3, Exit 5), the royal throne hall (Geunjeongjeon) and water pavilions have anchored Kingdom, The Red Sleeve, Under the Queen's Umbrella, and BTS's iconic 'IDOL' & 'Mikrokosmos' performances on The Tonight Show.

Half-Day Walking Itinerary & Subway Navigation Strategy

Start at Gyeongbokgung Station (Exit 4) at 9:00 AM when palace gates open. Wearing a rented traditional Hanbok grants you free admission to all major royal palaces in Seoul. Spend 90 minutes photographing Geunjeongjeon Hall and Gyeonghoeru Pavilion before exiting via the east gate toward Bukchon Hanok Village.

From Bukchon, stroll downhill into Ikseon-dong Hanok Alley (익선동 한옥거리) for lunch—where early-1920s courtyard houses have been transformed into sunlit soufflé pancake cafes, salt-bread bakeries, and craft tea houses featured in Hotel Del Luna and Queen of Tears. Finish your afternoon with a 15-minute walk along Cheonggyecheon Stream to Deoksugung Stone Wall Road at golden hour.

Where to Eat, Stay & Book Local Passes in Jongno

Local Culinary Highlights: Don't miss Tosokchon Samgyetang (ginseng chicken soup inside a grand hanok near Gyeongbokgung), Hwangsaengga Kalguksu (Michelin Bib Gourmand hand-cut noodle soup and giant steamed dumplings in Bukchon), and Onion Anguk for pastries under a traditional wooden roof.

Where to Stay: Choosing a hotel in Insadong, Myeongdong, or Gwanghwamun puts you within walking distance of all four royal palaces and direct Airport Railroad / Limousine Bus connections to Incheon International Airport (ICN).
